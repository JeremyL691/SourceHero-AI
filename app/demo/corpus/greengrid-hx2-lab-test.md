GreenGrid Review obtained three HX-2 prototype cells from Halcyon Cells and tested them over eleven weeks in an independent laboratory. The results do not match the company's own figures.

Independent lab tests measured the HX-2 energy density at 380 Wh/kg.

That is roughly 16% below the 450 Wh/kg that Halcyon has been quoting, and closer to what other solid-state programmes have achieved so far.

The HX-2 retained 92% of its capacity after 1,000 charge cycles.

Cycling performance, by contrast, was better than expected. Most competing prototypes in the same class lost between 70% and 85% of initial capacity over the same number of cycles.

Halcyon Cells did not respond to a request for comment, but a spokesperson said the cells used in production will differ from the prototypes supplied to reviewers.

The laboratory noted that its measurements follow the standard IEC 62660 procedure, and that cell-level energy density is measured at a lower cut-off voltage than pack-level figures.
