Halcyon Cells showed off its new HX-2 solid-state cell at a press event in San Jose on Monday, promising a leap in energy density over the lithium-ion cells that dominate today's EV market.

The HX-2 cell delivers an energy density of 450 Wh/kg.

Halcyon said the cell uses a proprietary ceramic electrolyte that it has been developing since 2021. Engineers familiar with the programme described the chemistry as unusually stable at high temperatures, which is where earlier solid-state designs have tended to fail.

Mass production of the HX-2 will begin in September 2026.

The company plans to build the first line at a site it acquired last autumn, and said it has already hired most of the operators needed for a pilot run.

Halcyon Cells expects HX-2 pack prices of $120/kWh at launch.

Analysts were split on that figure. One battery economist put the realistic first-year number closer to $160/kWh, arguing that ceramic separator yields will be the limiting factor in the early years.
